I really hate complicated RPG's sometimes because you gotta COMMIT and envelop your life into learning it and keeping track of everything. It's a lot of fun in the moment.
But then when I eventually drop the game for a few months and then return, the re-learning process is exhausting. What I want is a more casual-based RPG. Something with 
baked-in familiarity and accessibility, while still allowing for for synergies and shenanigans. A bite-sized roguelike RPG where runs can be as complicated (or not) as you want.

I think this could be a blend of a few different games:
1. Gumballs and Dungeons - a dungeon-crawler like game with lots of encounters, characters, variety, etc.
2. Chess - you know it, you love it
3. D&D/Baldurs Gate - turn-based RPG

It sounds like a lot. But here's the basics

---MVP---
1. You control a chess piece of your own choosing and are versus an enemy AI with their own pieces.
2. Pieces have movements reminiscent of chess, but can also attack or use abilities.
3. Your goal is to eliminate the opponent from the board/map and progress to the next board
4. There is a set number of boards and you must clear all of them before you "win"

---Extras (if MVP turns out well)---
1. You can find items/modifiers for your piece during its run
2. non-chess pieces (ships from Battleship, Monopoly pieces, checkers, etc?)
3. chess piece variants
